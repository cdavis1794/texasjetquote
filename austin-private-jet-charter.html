Austin airports used for private flights

Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) is the region’s primary commercial airport and also supports general aviation. It may be convenient when passengers are connecting to airline service. Austin Executive Airport (EDC), northeast of central Austin, is dedicated to general aviation and can be practical for private-flight access. Availability, runway suitability, operating conditions and fees must be confirmed for the specific aircraft and date.

Planning tip: ask the quote provider to compare the all-in trip—not just airtime—when considering AUS versus EDC. Ground time, aircraft positioning and airport fees can outweigh a small mileage difference.

What affects an Austin charter quote?

  1. Aircraft positioning: the closest suitable aircraft may need to fly empty before or after your trip.
  2. Aircraft category: passenger count, baggage, runway performance and desired cabin determine the realistic options.
  3. Schedule: exact departure windows, crew duty limits and overnight requirements affect availability.
  4. Airport costs: handling, landing, parking and other charges vary by airport and itinerary.
  5. Trip structure: a same-day return, multi-day stay and one-way mission can require different operating plans.

Information to include in your request

  • preferred city pair or airport codes;
  • departure date, time window and flexibility;
  • passenger count, ages, pets and accessibility needs;
  • quantity and size of baggage;
  • one-way, same-day return or multi-day itinerary;
  • cabin, catering and ground-transfer preferences.

Verify the flight before paying

Ask who will exercise operational control and obtain the operator’s legal name and certificate information. The FAA advises charter customers to identify the actual operator and check its authorization. A marketplace or referral website may introduce options, but it does not operate the aircraft.
